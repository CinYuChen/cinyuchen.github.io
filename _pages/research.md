# What I Enjoy About Probability

I enjoy abstract mathematics for its own sake, but what makes probability especially interesting to me is that many abstract concepts gain an additional layer of interpretation. For example, a sigma-algebra can be viewed as information, and conditional expectation can be understood as prediction based on the available information. I find this extra layer of meaning especially appealing.

I first came to appreciate the elegance of martingale theory while studying David Williams' Probability with Martingales. I was fascinated by how such a simple probabilistic structure could lead to powerful arguments and simplify seemingly complicated situation. For instance, martingales satisfy remarkable convergence theorems under relatively mild conditions, and obtain good properties while interacting with stopping times. I find it beautiful that a simple defining property can have such far-reaching consequences.

# My Interest in Mathematical Finance

After several years of theoretical study, I became increasingly interested in exploring how probability theory could help us understand concrete problems. During my research assistantship, I began studying mathematical finance as another setting in which to explore the applications of probability.

What attracts me most to mathematical finance is not simply the calculation of prices, but how financial intuition brings probabilistic ideas to life, and how probability theory, in turn, provides a rigorous framework for financial concepts.

For instance, I was fascinated by the connection between martingales and the no-arbitrage principle. Both seem to capture a certain sense of fairness from different angles, collaborating to construct a coherent pricing framework.

As I studied these ideas, I was naturally curious about :
"Why can this mathematical quantity be interpreted as a price?"
Moreover, it crossed my mind that "What do we gain by expressing a price, initially derived from a replicating trading strategy, as an expectation under an equivalent martingale measure?" and "How does this change in perspective help us to develop deeper?"

These kind of questions led me to compare different treatments of the same topic, trying to understand how their perspectives complement one another.

For example, when studying American contingent claims, I noticed that R. J. Williams develops the pricing problem from the seller's perspective through superhedging, whereas Shreve emphasizes the buyer's optimal exercise problem. Comparing these approaches helped me appreciate how different viewpoints can illuminate different aspects of the same mathematical problem.

Rather than focusing solely on individual statements and proofs, I enjoy tracing why concepts are introduced, how theorems relate to one another, and how they fit together into a coherent theoretical framework.

# Looking Ahead

Every time I study mathematical concepts like martingales, I feel motivated to learn more. While I enjoy learning established mathematical theories, I hope to move beyond textbooks and engage with research problems. I would like to develop the ability not only to understand existing results, but also to formulate meaningful questions and contribute to their investigation.

This is my main motivation for pursuing a Ph.D. I look forward to working in a research environment where I can exchange ideas, learn from experienced researchers, and gradually develop my own approach to mathematical research.
